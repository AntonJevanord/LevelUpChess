# Version-17 human playtest notes

## 28 October 2026: pawn development and defensive threats

The player reported that a King-step pawn near the king was awkward to activate
while keeping the king's pawn cover. Moving it to the queenside gave it more
freedom. In the supplied game, b4–c4–b3 used its sideways and backward mobility;
`11.Vxc4 dxc4 12.Bxc4` exchanged it for two ordinary pawns.

The player also observed that the Two-step pawn protected another pawn and
deterred pressure through the threat of recapture. From c2 it can defend e4
through a clear d3 square. **A capture that never happens can still matter.**
Counting unusual moves played is therefore a poor proxy for an upgrade's value.
Review supported pawn chains, protected squares and attacks the opponent avoids,
alongside development time and king safety.

In the second supplied game, `15.A@f4` threatened the f5-pawn. After
`…e4 16.dxe4 fxe4`, the target had moved to e4, outside the grant's capture
direction. Its forward mobility offered little help against the later attack
near the king. Black's own grant remained in reserve. This illustrates placement
and timing, rather than establishing a balance change from one result.

These are qualitative observations from two user-supplied games, not additional
engine-match evidence. Keep the current movements while comparing the selected
references in the [matchup panel](reference-panel-v17.md).
